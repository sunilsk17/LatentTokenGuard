# Earlier experiments: POPE

These are the results of the first version of LatentTokenGuard, which scored content tokens in
answers to POPE yes/no questions. They are kept for reference. The current evaluation is the
free-form caption benchmark described in the [README](README.md) and in
[docs/CAPTION_BENCHMARK.md](docs/CAPTION_BENCHMARK.md): POPE answers are a single "yes" or "no",
so a detector can score well on POPE by reflecting the model's own answer rather than its visual
grounding, which the caption benchmark rules out.

## Metrics (POPE-adversarial, 3,000 questions)

Layer 15 activations, 4-bit NF4 weights, fixed random seed. Thresholds were tuned for F1.

| Model | Threshold (τ) | Accuracy | F1 | AUROC | Latency (ms) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| LLaVA-1.5-7B | 0.150 | 69.67% | 0.7415 | 0.7827 | ~747 |
| Phi-3.5-Vision | 0.090 | 51.70% | 0.6727 | 0.5078 | ~599 |

Latency is total inference time (generation and detection) on one NVIDIA A100 (40 GB).

## Environment

- GPU: NVIDIA A100 (40 GB)
- `torch`, `transformers==4.43.0`, `bitsandbytes` (4-bit NF4)
- `spacy` with `en_core_web_sm` for content-token selection

## Observation

LLaVA-1.5 maps image patches into the language model through an MLP projector that keeps their
spatial layout, and its alignment scores separate grounded from hallucinated tokens. Phi-3.5-Vision
compresses the image more aggressively (HD transform), and plain cosine alignment is close to chance
on it.

## Replication

```bash
python main.py --model llava --dataset pope --split adversarial --threshold 0.150 --mode alignment_only
```
